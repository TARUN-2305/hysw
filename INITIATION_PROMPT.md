# Initiation Prompt — Start Phase 1 (Research + Feasibility)

Read `CLAUDE.md` completely and treat it as the standing project instructions. The two attached syllabus screenshots are the course reference.

We are beginning **Phase 1 only: Research, project selection and feasibility validation** for the CS372IA Experiential Learning Project.

Your task is to independently discover and select one grounded, creative, technically defensible and feasible parallel-computing project that can score well against the course's experiential-learning rubric. The project's central contribution must genuinely use parallel computing and map clearly to the supplied syllabus.

Do not start with a long list of generic ideas. First inspect the current workspace and actual development environment. Then identify at most three promising candidates, conduct focused prior-art and technical research, and compare them consistently. Select the strongest candidate yourself; do not ask me to choose unless an important constraint cannot be discovered or reasonably assumed.

**Important: research includes experiments.** When an assumption can be tested cheaply, test it. You may create small, disposable proof-of-concept scripts, smoke tests, microbenchmarks, tiny kernels, sample algorithm implementations or dataset checks to establish feasibility. In particular, validate the riskiest technical assumption rather than simply saying that it should work. Keep these experiments small and clearly labelled as exploratory. Do not build the complete project during this phase.

Be economical with time and credits: use a focused set of high-quality sources, avoid repeated searches and unnecessary subagents, inspect before installing, do not download large datasets or weights prematurely, and do not use paid services. Do not fabricate evidence or benchmark results.

Create the Phase 1 artifacts specified in `CLAUDE.md`, especially:
- candidate comparison;
- research findings and source register;
- environment and feasibility experiment log;
- final project decision with a precise contribution and scope;
- actionable Phase 2 architecture and implementation blueprint;
- Phase 1 exit checklist.

Your final recommendation must answer, with evidence:
1. What exact problem are we solving, and for whom or what use case?
2. What is the proposed contribution, and how is it different from the most relevant existing approaches?
3. Why is parallel computing necessary or beneficial for this workload?
4. Which course concepts and programming model does it demonstrate?
5. What did you actually test, on what environment, and what did the tests show?
6. What remains uncertain, and what fallback addresses it?
7. How will correctness and performance be evaluated fairly?
8. What exactly will be built in Phase 2, in what order, and what will the final academic submission contain?

**Stop condition:** Once the Phase 1 exit gate in `CLAUDE.md` is satisfied, stop. Do not begin the full implementation, polished UI, final report or presentation. Give me the selected project, the evidence supporting it, the feasibility results, the remaining risks and the Phase 2 blueprint. Wait for my explicit instruction to begin Phase 2.

Begin now. Be decisive, evidence-led and cost-conscious.
